# Professional Minecraft Mod Development Practices and What Makes a Mod Fun

Research report, 2026-10-31. [V] = verified from a cited source this session; [I] = inference or general knowledge not fetched.

# Part 1 — How professional Minecraft mod teams actually work

## 1.1 Asset pipeline

**Who makes the art.** Large mods keep a small, stable artist roster rather than commissioning per feature. Create credits four artists (Kryppers, oaklineer, LeastReality, vectorwing) separately from seven developers [V, https://modrinth.com/mod/create]. vectorwing is also the sole author of Farmer's Delight, so one artist's palette spans two of the most vanilla-feeling mods [V, https://github.com/vectorwing/FarmersDelight]. Alex's Mobs states every design, model, texture and animation is "the work of AlexModGuy and the Alex's Mobs team" [V, https://www.modpackindex.com/mod/163637/alexs-mobs-continued]. Biomes O' Plenty's credits split Forstride (art, biome design) from Adubbz (code) [V, 2013-era source credits]. When Dungeons Arise names three primary builders (Aurelj, DiamondTown, Zephyrusj) plus two guest builders whose structures were included by permission [V, https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/when-dungeons-arise]. Cobblemon recruits modelers through Discord and an "application-box" forum; casual contributors fork and pick up "accepted"-labelled GitLab issues [V, https://gitlab.com/cable-mc/cobblemon]. Inference: the common pattern is one or two style owners with veto power, not a marketplace [I].

**Tools.** Blockbench is universal for models and animation (named by Cobblemon FAQ answers and by the awesome-packdev list); Aseprite or LibreSprite for textures [V, https://github.com/Modern-Modpacks/awesome-packdev]. Blockbench's Minecraft style guide is the closest thing to a written vanilla art spec: build palettes from a midtone plus one shadow and one highlight, hue-shift ramps (straight ramps "look dull"), pencil and fill only (no soft brushes), light from top-left on items with a "significantly darker outline", top and front brighter than bottom and back on entities, minimal element counts, no stair-stepped slants, test tiling on a 3x3 wall early. Named failure modes: banding, pillow shading, pancake shading, mixels, jaggies, brush noise ("noise adds no information to the texture") [V, https://blockbench.net/wiki/guides/minecraft-style-guide]. Faithful's texturing rules add the practical consistency test: view from a distance next to vanilla, "the less difference, the better" [V, https://docs.faithfulpack.net/pages/textures/cf32-texturing-guidelines]. Cobblemon's CI has a dedicated PackSquash stage that compresses assets before the Gradle build [V, .gitlab-ci.yml].

**Structures.** No team publishes a step-by-step build workflow. Verifiable facts: YUNG's repos store structures as data (`structure/`, `worldgen/` template pools and processors, `loot_table/`, `advancement/`, per-loader `biome_modifier/`) in a Common/Fabric/NeoForge layout [V, https://github.com/YUNG-GANG/YUNGs-Better-Dungeons]. YUNG's stated rule: "Most of my structures are designed gameplay-first... I primarily focus on ensuring that traversing the structure is a fun experience for the player", aesthetics second, multiple approaches rewarded [V, https://blog.curseforge.com/10-questions-with-yung/]. Twilight Forest keeps NBTs under `data/twilightforest/structure` and licenses structures and sounds "All Rights Reserved" separately from code [V, https://github.com/TeamTwilight/twilightforest]. Stardust Labs (Terralith, Incendium, Structory) builds everything as datapacks with vanilla blocks only, uses per-MC-version overlay folders, gates experimental builds to Patreon/boosters, and forbids AI training in its license [V, https://github.com/Stardust-Labs-MC/Terralith, https://stardustlabs.miraheze.org/wiki/Main_page]. IDAS is a rework of WDA's structures "enriching them with blocks, decorations and loot from Quark, Create, Supplementaries" [V]. Palette-first building is supported by tooling (Palettinator scans a structure's most-used blocks into a hotbar palette) but no team states it as process [I].

## 1.2 Testing and QA

**GameTests are standard among engineering-heavy mods.**

- Mekanism: source sets `additions`, `api`, `datagen`, `gameTest`, `generators`, `integration`, `main`, `test`, `tools`; a `gametests.yml` workflow runs `runGameTestServer` then `test` on Java 25 (Temurin) for every push and every PR touching `**.java`, 60-minute timeout, results only in logs. Other workflows: `codeql.yml`, `crowdin_download.yml`, `crowdin_source_upload.yml`, `gradle-publish.yml` [V, https://github.com/mekanism/Mekanism/tree/26.3].
- AE2: `build.yml` runs `runData` and fails if generated files differ from committed ones, then `build publish`, then `runGametest`, merges JUnit XML into one report, uploads to Crowdin on main, publishes snapshots to Maven Central. Other workflows: `release.yml`, `test-report.yml`, `export_guide.yml`, `crowdin-upload-guidebook.yml`, `localization.yml`. Its `AGENTS.md` tells agents to prefer the IDE MCP server, explains the "Components not bound yet" unit-test failure (inject a MinecraftServer via `@ExtendWith(EphemeralTestServerProvider.class)`), and points to `appeng.server.testplots` for gametests; `CLAUDE.md` is one line, `@AGENTS.md` [V, https://github.com/AppliedEnergistics/Applied-Energistics-2]. CONTRIBUTING: open an issue first, use the Eclipse formatter via `spotlessApply`, "Always fully test your changes. If they are large enough in scope, then fully test AE2", describe the testing process in the PR, syntax-only or clean-up PRs rejected [V, https://github.com/AppliedEnergistics/Applied-Energistics-2/blob/main/.github/CONTRIBUTING.md].
- Botania: GameTests live in `src/main/java/vazkii/botania/test`, registered via the `fabric-gametest` entrypoint; "All tests should size themselves appropriately to ensure they don't interfere with other tests"; "keep 5 blocks of padding in the N/S/E/W directions of all tests that have mana pools, spreaders, or flowers"; batches run in parallel; structure files in `data/botania/gametest/structures` with air removed; "for gameplay changes, consider adding a GameTest" [V, https://github.com/VazkiiMods/Botania/blob/1.20.x/CONTRIBUTING.md].
- Fabric official guidance: fabric-loader-junit for unit tests (call `SharedConstants.tryDetectVersion()` and `Bootstrap.bootStrap()` for registry-touching code), server gametests run under `build`, client gametests via `runProductionClientGameTest` with XVFB on Linux CI and screenshot artifacts, upload `build/reports/` on failure [V, https://docs.fabricmc.net/develop/automatic-testing]. NeoForge docs: `test_instance` JSON per test, environments drive batching, `runGameTestServer`, "The build server returns an exit code of the number of required, failed Game Tests" [V, https://docs.neoforged.net/docs/misc/gametest].
- Counter-examples: Cobblemon's CI has no test job (a comment says the client reference test process is a TODO) [V]; Create, Botania and Ars Nouveau use Jenkins (`Jenkinsfile` present); Twilight Forest uses Azure Pipelines [V]. Inference: automated testing correlates with mods whose core is simulation logic (AE2, Mekanism, Botania), not content mods [I].

**Datagen is a build artifact, not hand-written JSON.** AE2's CI drift check is the strongest example [V]; Mekanism has a whole `datagen` source set [V]; Balm's template ships a "verify-resources" CI job that errors if `runData` was not run [V, https://balm.twelveiterations.com/getting-started/datagen].

**Human QA channels.** Twilight Forest ships experimental builds via Discord [V]. mDiyo describes the original Tinkers loop as a forum thread cycle ("putting something out, getting thoughts, bringing those back into the design"), now Discord, "faster but less considered" [V, https://blog.curseforge.com/10-questions-with-mdiyo/]. Vazkii: releases publish automatically to GitHub, CurseForge and Modrinth, signed with a trusted key; Violet Moon team maintains while he acts as creative lead [V, https://blog.curseforge.com/10-questions-with-vazkii/]. Ars Nouveau publishes every push to the BlameJared Maven [V, https://github.com/baileyholl/Ars-Nouveau].

## 1.3 Process, scope, versioning

- **Scope gates are explicit and harsh.** Botania: "Suggestions are **not** accepted... The bar for new features is quite high", keep PRs small, one fix per PR, changelog entry under "Upcoming" [V]. AE2: suggestions already possible in vanilla or AE2 are invalid, as are requests for "smaller, more compact, or more efficient" versions [V]. Tinkers: "that feature should have multiple uses. Try and come up with 3 usecases for the idea"; "We will often critique your idea, so do not make suggestions if you are not open to criticism"; compat only if it covers many mods, is trivial, the maintainer plays it, or collaborates with its author; "You should never write a mixin into another mod until you talked to the mod's author" [V, https://slimeknights.github.io/faq/]. Vazkii: feature choice is "entirely vibe-based"; "If you make something everyone likes, you make something no one loves"; keeping the vision was "paramount" to Botania's survival; "Any attempt at making a thing to be popular or gain income from is a waste" [V].
- **Design docs are rare but exist.** Tinkers maintains public design docs (how to select numbers for stat traits, modifiers vs enchantments, material tiers, alloy chart) and warns "The in game books remain the best source of information accurate to your version of the mod" [V, https://slimeknights.github.io/docs/design/]. mDiyo on TC3: "It needed to be 'Minecraft,' not a mod or an experience", considering builders, explorers, speedrunners and casual players at each tier; each mod started from "some part of it grinds my gears in a bad way, and I want to fix it" [V].
- **Code conventions.** Twilight Forest: prefer vanilla methods over copying; if copying, copy exactly and mark the class `[VanillaCopy]` so it is re-reviewed on updates; delete dead and commented code ("Git exists for a reason") [V, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TeamTwilight/twilightforest/1.21.x/CONTRIBUTING.md].
- **Versioning.** AE2 uses semver since 1.15 ("A change of the MAJOR version will be an API break") [V]. Create and Mekanism use per-MC-version branches (`mc1.21.1/dev`, `26.3`) [V]. Multi-loader via Common/Fabric/NeoForge layouts (YUNG, Supplementaries), Xplat/Fabric/Forge (Botania), Architectury (Cobblemon, Kotlin) [V]. McJty says the loader split "fractures the Minecraft modding community" and he has stopped updating most mods to the latest MC, focusing on the popular ones; McJtyLib exists so "common code only needs to be ported once" [V, https://blog.curseforge.com/10-questions-with-mcjty/].
- **Balance lives in data.** Tinkers: "We plan to continue migrating features to data packs"; nearly all recipes changeable, removing a recipe effectively removes a feature; low-code JSON addons [V]. McJty: "I'm a strong believer in configurability and giving the players a choice" (In Control! JSON language, Lost Cities datapacks) [V]. FTB packs tune via KubeJS; FTB Quests fires KubeJS events and uses KubeJS or Game Stages as its stages backend through FTB XMod Compat, falling back to player tags [V, https://www.modpackindex.com/mod/45936/ftb-xmod-compat]. FTB Academy credits show a role split: quests and guide by aaronhowser1, backend by Sunekaer, mod work by LatvianModder [V, https://breezewiki.discard.no/ftb/wiki/FTB_Academy]. Pack tooling in common use: KubeJS, CraftTweaker, GroovyScript, Custom Machinery, ProbeJS, Patchouli plus Writouli web editor, Spark profiler, Packwiz/Pakku packaging [V, awesome-packdev].
- **Localization** is Crowdin everywhere (Create, Mekanism, AE2, TF, Ars Nouveau; AE2 requires UTF-8 and intact `%s` placeholders) [V].

## 1.4 Player-facing conventions that have become standard

- **In-game docs:** Patchouli (Botania's Lexica Botania), AE2's own guidebook (exported and translated by CI, hosted at guide.appliedenergistics.org), Create's Ponder ("will guide you through all added mechanics and gadgets"). Ponder scenes are code: prepare a schematic under the Ponder resource folder, write a static method taking `SceneBuilder` and `SceneBuildingUtil`, reload with Shift+S; scenes are "packed and baked into each version of the Create mod" [V, https://github.com/Creators-of-Create/Create/wiki/Internal---Ponder-UI]. Ponder is now a standalone library for Forge/Fabric/NeoForge on 1.20.1 and 1.21.1 [V, https://github.com/Creators-of-Create/Ponder]; Tinkers has a Ponder integration; the third-party Ponderer adds an in-game scene editor, PonderJS conversion, and an optional LLM draft generator [V, https://modrinth.com/mod/the-ponderer].
- **QoL baseline in packs:** JEI or EMI (some packs run both with a bridge), Jade, AppleSkin, Mouse Tweaks, Enchantment Descriptions, Xaero's Minimap, Inventory Profiles Next [V, https://blog.curseforge.com/best-minecraft-quality-of-life-mods/ and pack listings]. No formal "first join" standard exists; FTB Quests with its in-game editor and shareable SNBT files is the de facto onboarding tool [V].
- **Vanilla-plus positioning language:** Supplementaries "fills the gaps vanilla has... functional, aesthetically viable, and complementary to preexisting content"; Farmer's Delight "gently expands upon farming and cooking" [V].

## 1.5 AI and LLM tooling in the ecosystem, 2025 to 2026

- Create's `AGENTS.md` is a "no LLM contributions" notice that "discourages the use of LLM tools for even basic comprehension of the code, debugging, testing" and instructs agents to warn and decline all work [V, https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Creators-of-Create/Create/mc1.21.1/dev/AGENTS.md]. AE2 does the opposite with an `AGENTS.md` of MCP and test guidance imported by `CLAUDE.md` [V].
- Modrinth (announced Aug 13 2026, enforced after a grace period ending Sep 27 2026): "Projects may not be published publicly if the contents are primarily or entirely a product of AI output"; AI images banned on project pages; a "Contains AI-generated content" disclosure (sub-options code, assets, text, functionality) required when a substantial portion of code, any assets, functionality or page text is AI output; Terms forbid using the site to train models; users who harass over disclosures are banned [V, https://modrinth.com/news/article/ai-policy-and-disclosures/]. Rationale given: submissions rose from about 2,500 to over 5,000 per week.
- CurseForge: no AI policy found [V, absence]. Nexus Mods uses AI tags with penalties for lying [V]. Mojang pledged no generative AI in Minecraft development (Larsson: "it is important that it makes us feel happy to create as humans") [V]. Terralith's license bars AI training [V].
- Found only vendor tooling (Claude Code plugins, no-code generators, Ponderer's LLM option), no team describing LLM use in its own workflow beyond AE2's agent file [V].

## 1.6 What Part 1 implies for a repeatable AI-driven process

1. **Datagen plus a drift check in CI** (AE2 pattern) is the single highest-value habit: no hand JSON, and the build fails if generated output isn't committed.
2. **Three test tiers are the industry ceiling, not the floor**: JUnit with bootstrap, server GameTests with explicit batching and padding rules, optional client gametests. Lord of the Lands already matches best-in-class; the gap teams have is content mods with zero tests.
3. **Write Tinkers-style design docs and a scope gate** ("3 use cases", "not achievable in vanilla", "fits the vision") that an agent must satisfy before code. Keep in-game text as the version-accurate source and docs as intent.
4. **Codify art rules as a lint**: the Blockbench style guide is specific enough to turn into a checklist (ramp size, light direction, outline, tiling test). Appoint one human style owner with veto.
5. **Structures are data**: template pools, processors, loot, advancement, built gameplay-first (traversal, multiple routes). Build a palette reference before the first room.
6. **Automate release and localization** (signed multi-platform publish, Crowdin sync) so the human loop is design and playtest only.
7. **Disclosure is now a platform requirement** (Modrinth). Record AI involvement per asset and commit so disclosure is mechanical, and respect per-project refusals like Create's.

# Part 2 — What makes a mod and a game fun

## 2.1 General frameworks, compressed to decision rules

- **MDA** (Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek 2004): mechanics give rise to dynamics which give rise to aesthetics; designers control mechanics but players experience aesthetics, so evaluate every mechanic by the dynamic it produces [V, https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf]. LeBlanc's eight aesthetics: sensation, fantasy, narrative, challenge, fellowship, discovery, expression, submission [V, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MDA_framework].
- **Self-Determination Theory / PENS** (Ryan, Rigby, Przybylski 2006): competence, autonomy, relatedness predict sustained play [V, https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/].
- **Koster, A Theory of Fun**: "Fun in games arises out of mastery. It arises out of comprehension... With games, learning is the drug"; once patterns are mastered the challenge becomes trivial and boredom follows unless patterns are re-presented in novel ways [V, https://www.raphkoster.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Theory-of-Fun-10-Years-Later.pdf]. This is the mechanism behind the content treadmill; "hedonic adaptation" is my framing [I].
- **Flow** (Csikszentmihalyi) and **Schell's lenses**: not fetched this session; standard references [I].
- **Teaching without tutorials**: GMTK's Half-Life 2 "invisible tutorial" (present a situation before teaching the tool, e.g. the Ravenholm sawblade demonstration) [V, https://www.thegamer.com/half-life-does-tutorials-right/]. George Fan's Plants vs Zombies GDC talk: "The best way for a player to learn is to actually perform actions in the game", spread mechanics across levels, "Eight words on the screen at any given time" [V, https://www.aol.com/2012/03/09/plants-vs-zombies-casual-gamers-strategy-game/]. **Kishōtenketsu** (Hayashida, Super Mario 3D World): introduction, development, twist, resolution per level [V, secondary via https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-nintendos-level-design-secrets-in-four-steps].
- **Juice**: Vlambeer's "The Art of Screenshake" (INDIGO Classes 2013) and Jonasson/Purho's "Juice it or lose it": feedback density makes a mechanic feel alive; Nijman cautions it can cost immersion [V, secondary, https://rpgplayground.com/research-making-a-juicy-game/].
- **Clarity**: Burgun argues for one strong core mechanism and removing conflicting elements; "When adding 'more' to a game you decrease the likelihood of being able to balance the game" [V, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-how-i-auro-i-seeks-to-simplify-the-roguelike]. Depth vs complexity: "Something complex is hard to learn, something deep is hard to master" [V, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/design-101-complexity-vs-depth]. Jonathan Blow's talks not retrieved [I].
- **Second-order optimal strategy / "players will optimize the fun out of a game"**: commonly attributed to Soren Johnson; primary source not confirmed today [I, unverified].

## 2.2 Minecraft-specific wisdom

**Mojang's own rules** from Jeb's internal design book, as summarized by a secondary source [V, https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/minecraft/]: "One block at a time!" (no copy-paste or templates; players learn by watching others); "There is no Steve or Alex"; "Not quite an RPG" (progression through items and knowledge, not levels); "Bad things happen..." (failure from player action, dying still fun); "You are not wearing a hud" (minimal UI, show rather than tell); "Finding things is fun. Searching for them is not." (hidden rewards reachable, e.g. maps); "It's up to the players to build the world"; "Death is real"; "Minecraft is not an editor"; "Bugs are not features"; "Expand the mysteries"; "Keep it vanilla"; items serve a purpose, new features respect existing ones, mobs have personality, hostile mobs unique. Anecdote: redstone repeater delays changed to 1, 2, 3, 4 because they were easier to understand. Larsson: "if the game gets too template and predetermined, you risk killing the creative joy"; Minecraft is a game where players set their own goals [V, https://liu.se/en/news-item/gladje-och-kreativitet-ledord-for-agnes-larsson]. Mojang's "no Minecraft 2, at least 15 more years" stance means vanilla parity is a moving target [V, https://sportskeeda.com/minecraft/minecraft-2-happening-mojang-plans-keep-game-alive-another-15-years].

**Why the admired mods work, developer statements where available:**

- Create: "The added elements of tech are designed to leave as many design choices to the player as possible. With Create, the game isn't played inside a bunch of UIs, it challenges you to set up contraptions of animated components"; Ponder as show-don't-tell [V, https://github.com/Creators-of-Create/Create]. Visible motion is the feedback channel, matching Jeb's HUD rule [I].
- Botania: automation "without elements that make it feel grindy"; systems should be planned rather than copied from JEI or a wiki; Vazkii's 2015 essay against passive mana generation concludes "game design should not purely focus on ease and power, but engagement" [V, https://vazkii.notion.site/Sins-of-a-Solar-Empire-or-The-Passive-Generation-Conundrum-c5971117fd3648139a4a078541096fac, snippet only].
- Tinkers: three uses per feature; four player archetypes per tier; modifiers deliberately distinct from enchantments [V].
- YUNG: structures gameplay-first, traversal and multiple approaches, skill or creativity rewarded [V].
- SevTech: Ages: "long term progression experience with purpose", "hiding ore until unlocked", recipes revealed dynamically, gating via vanilla advancements. Reviews praise a unique early game ("caveman to spacefaring") and say age 4 loses exploration and "Age 5 isn't worth playing" [V, https://moddex.gg/modpack/sevtech-ages/reviews/1068, https://mmcreviews.com/all/modpacks/sevtech-ages/]. Lesson: legible progression works until the novelty curve flattens [I].
- Vault Hunters: roguelite loop (enter vault, fight, loot, die, grow via talents and abilities), vault entry has a resource cost, end goal of 25 artifacts and a final boss; a 3rd-edition review calls it "lacking a feeling of progression" with "unnecessarily grindy" recipes [V, https://mmcreviews.com/all/modpacks/vault-hunters-3rd-edition].
- Feed the Factory, praised as "one of the exemplars in how to design a tech-centered modpack": "all gameplay elements not related to the tech premise are removed, and the progression is strictly linear" [V, https://moddex.gg/modpack/feed-the-factory/reviews/109].
- Thaumcraft and Terrafirmacraft: only low-authority third-party characterizations found (TFC: realism and difficulty as the point, ages gated by real technology) [V, low authority].

**Failure modes, with evidence:**

- Grind as content substitute: "Major part: cut the grind. Reduce the cost of making things like iron down from 16" [V, https://moddex.gg/modpack/universio/reviews/181]; thin loops ("more to the progression than the ore-for-better-ore cycle") [V].
- Gating in dead space with few block types, filler enemies, early mistakes that go unnoticed for hours "which feels terrible" [V, Moddex reviews].
- Text-dump guidebooks: dislike of mods that "throw a book with all the knowledge in the UNIVERSE at you"; ore doubling and grinders should be "signals for succeeding in mastering the mods mechanics not the primary purpose" [V, https://forum.feed-the-beast.com/threads/discussion-on-mod-design-progression-management.148498/post-1621857].
- Datapacks used as "glorified config files" rather than design [V, reddit mirror].
- Menu or spreadsheet play, invisible mechanics, undiscovered features, chat-message reliance: widely discussed but no single authoritative source found; inferred from Jeb's HUD rule and Create's stated contrast [I].
- Feature bloat: Burgun's balance argument plus Botania and AE2 refusal policies are the implicit counter-rubric [V/I].

**Published rubrics:** none from Modrinth or CurseForge; their rules concern content policy, not design quality [V]. The closest operational rubrics are maintainer intake rules (Tinkers' three uses, AE2's "already possible in vanilla" rejection, Botania's high bar) and Blockbench's art checklist [V].

## 2.3 What Part 2 implies for a repeatable AI-driven process

Turn the above into a pre-build gate and a post-build review that an agent fills in:

1. **Loop statement**: name the 30-second, 10-minute and session loops, and which LeBlanc aesthetic and PENS need each serves. A feature serving none fails Tinkers' three-use test.
2. **Minecraft-parity check**: does it add a HUD, play itself, bulk-place blocks, level the player, depend on respawn, or duplicate a vanilla capability? Reject per AE2's rule.
3. **Discoverability plan**: how the player learns it in-world before any book. Require a kishōtenketsu-style first encounter (see it work, try it safely, twist, payoff) via a Ponder-style scene or advancement, never chat text. Cap on-screen text near Fan's eight words.
4. **Feedback plan**: what the world (sound, particles, block state, mob behavior) does on success and failure, per Create. No feature ships with chat as its only feedback.
5. **Mastery curve**: what pattern is learned and what re-presents it later (Koster). "Same thing with bigger numbers" is treadmill, not depth.
6. **Grind audit**: wall-clock time to first payoff and to full capability versus a vanilla equivalent (diamond, Nether). Slower than vanilla needs a justification tied to a dynamic, not "balance".
7. **Second-order check**: enumerate the degenerate optimal strategy (passive farms, AFK loops) and either embrace it as a mastery signal (FTB thread) or design it out (Vazkii).
8. **Long-session playtest**: SevTech and Vault Hunters show late-game collapse is the common failure; schedule a long-session playtest, not just a first hour, and log where novelty flattens.
9. **Review against the failure list**: menu-only play, invisible mechanic, book-first teaching, chat-only feedback, scope creep (Burgun), vanilla-redundant.
